The recommendation

Río Guarapas at Pitalito 2

IDEAM 21017050 · Huila · Alto Magdalena · 1.8488 N, 76.0524 W

  • ✓Right size. 269 km², 48 km main channel, 1283–2908 m.a.s.l. Big enough for a real drainage network, small enough to stay hydrologically coherent.
  • ✓Nearly unbroken record. 40.7 observed years, 0.78% missing, longest single gap 62 days, and only 0.63% of days sitting in repeated-value runs.
  • ✓The forcing agrees with the gauge. Monthly rainfall and runoff are in phase at r = 0.88, and the annual runoff ratio varies by only 16% across four decades — the water balance closes.
  • ✓Real ground data inside the divide. Seven IDEAM stations in the basin, five still active, including two full climate stations — the densest of any shortlisted basin its size.
  • ✓Near-natural, and documented. No dam, 0.29% urban, headwaters inside PNN Cueva de los Guácharos, and an adopted POMCA with public diagnostic reports from CAM.

How the field narrowed

Five stages, 346 down to 5

Each stage tests something the next one depends on. Size and record length come from the dataset’s own metadata; the fourth stage is the one that did the real work, because it reads the daily series rather than trusting the summary columns.

  1. 01

    Everything gauged — 346

    All CAMELS-COL catchments: daily streamflow from IDEAM, CHIRPS rainfall and MSWX temperature and PET, 1981–2022, with 71 attributes each.

  2. 02

    Workable size — 259

    100–5000 km². Removes the 260,000 km² Magdalena main-stem gauges and a handful of plot-scale headwaters.

  3. 03

    Long, complete, unregulated — 95

    At least 25 observed years, record running to 2018 or later, 8% missing or less, runoff ratio between 0.20 and 0.95, under 2% urban and under 1.5% open water as a reservoir proxy.

  4. 04

    The daily series holds up — 12

    Computed from the raw files, not the metadata: longest gap 150 days or less, under 5% of days in flatlined runs, monthly rainfall–runoff correlation at least 0.60, seasonal phase at least 0.70, annual runoff-ratio CV no more than 0.30, and no step change over 20% between record halves.

  5. 05

    Supported on the ground — 5

    IDEAM rain and climate gauges counted inside each divide, plus a check for dams and published basin studies. This is where Río Opón fell out despite the best QC scores — one station inside 1786 km², and it is not active.

The test that mattered

A clean gauge record is not the same as a usable basin

The highest-scoring catchments on paper — Piendamó and Atá, both with 41-year records and under 2% missing — turned out to be unusable. Their streamflow peaks in June and July, exactly when CHIRPS says rainfall is at its annual minimum. Whatever is driving those rivers, the forcing data we would have to model them with does not see it.

Normalising each series to its own annual mean puts rainfall and runoff on one axis, so the only thing left to read is whether they rise and fall together.

The shortlist

Five basins worth the work

Ordered by overall fit. Every one of these passed all seven daily-series gates; they differ in size, in how natural the catchment is, and in how much ground instrumentation sits inside it.

Before you build on this

Three things wrong with the source data

All three were found while checking the dataset rather than reading about it. The first will silently corrupt any design-storm work if it goes unnoticed.

Bug

Do not use the published equi_slope or tc_* columns

CAMELS-COL reports channel slopes about 700× too small across all 346 catchments (median ratio 694 against a crude (Hmax−Hmin)/L estimate). Because Kirpich scales as S−0.385, that inflates every published concentration time by roughly 11×.

For Guarapas the file gives tc = 54.8 h; recomputing Kirpich from L = 48.2 km and S = 0.0337 gives 4.8 h, which is the physically sensible answer for a 269 km² Andean catchment. The tc_representative column is separately inconsistent — for Timba it reports 194.8 h, longer than any of the four equations it is supposed to select among. Recompute slope and tc from the DEM yourself.

Caveat

Rainfall is satellite, not gauge

Precipitation is CHIRPS v2 at 5 km and temperature and PET are MSWX at 0.1°, spatially averaged over each basin with no interpolation. In steep terrain CHIRPS can miss the local regime entirely — that is exactly what the phase test above detected. Use the IDEAM stations inside the basin to validate the forcing before calibrating anything, and treat any runoff ratio above 1.0 as a rainfall underestimate rather than a measurement error.

Caveat

Missing days are absent rows, and the docs disagree with the files

Gaps are dropped from the .txt series rather than written as null, so any naive read silently compresses the calendar and reports zero missing data. Reindex to a full daily range first. The bundled readme also variously claims 46, 192 and 921 catchments — the released tables contain 346 — and the land-cover file is row-per-gauge, not the column-per-gauge layout the readme describes.
